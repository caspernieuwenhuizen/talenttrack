The finished analysis — three ways to read it back

This is what the read-only view, the staff share link and the print sheet all render. Today every phase gets an identical full-width card, so a phase nobody rated takes as much room as the one with three points written about it, and the match itself — opponent, score, date — is a grey line above it all. Same content in all three; only the document changes.

1 — Report 2 — Scorecard 3 — Memo all three

1 — Report

Two columns on desktop: the match read on the left, the players on the right, so the two halves of the analysis are visible at once instead of one being a scroll away. Phases are rows, not cards — an unrated one costs a single grey line.

2 — Scorecard

The shape of the match first: six small tiles, one per phase, colour-carrying, readable in two seconds. Underneath, only the phases somebody actually wrote about. Players are grouped by what they were marked, which is the sentence a coach says out loud on Monday.

3 — Memo

Reads like something a person wrote. The coach's words lead at full size, the phases follow as a single quiet list with a coloured dot per rating, and the unrated ones are named once in a footnote rather than occupying the page. Least chrome of the three; best on paper.

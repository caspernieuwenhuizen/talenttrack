# Head coach — the season in actions

This is the working week of a head coach, in the order it actually happens.
Each action says what you are doing, where the screen is, and what it feeds
later. If you are new to TalentTrack, read [Getting started](getting-started.md)
first — this page assumes you can find the dashboard.

Everything here rolls up to one thing: the picture of each player. Attendance,
minutes, evaluations and notes are not admin. They are the evidence you will
lean on in a 1:1, a parent meeting, or a promotion call in eight months' time.

## Every training

### Record attendance

Open the training from your dashboard and mark who is there. On a phone, use
the attendance wizard — one tap per player, and it remembers where you were if
you get interrupted.

If you have a run of trainings to catch up on, the
[attendance grid](attendance-grid.md) does a whole period in one screen.

Attendance is the single most valuable thing you capture, because it is the
only field that tells you about a player when nothing else happened. A player
whose attendance quietly halves in October is a conversation you want to have
in October.

### Capture what you saw

The moment something stands out — a sharp first touch, a limp, body language
that is off — put a dated note on that player. Open the player and add a note;
it lands on their timeline.

Write it the same evening. A note written three days later is a summary; a
note written that night is evidence.

Notes marked **Internal notes (staff only)** are never shown to the player or
their parents. **Feedback for the player** is. Both live on the same screen, so
check which field you are typing into.

### Plan the next training

Build the training from the [exercise library](exercise-library.md), or reuse a
[training plan](training-plans.md) you have run before. Pick the methodology
principle you are working on so the training joins up with what you are
evaluating.

The [team planner](team-planner.md) shows the week as a whole — useful for
spotting the week where you have scheduled three trainings and a match.

## Every match

### Set the squad

Prepare the match from [match preparation](match-prep.md): pick your squad and
formation, assign roles, and print the team sheet if you want it on paper.

If you build line-ups regularly, save them as
[team blueprints](team-blueprint.md) so you are not rebuilding from scratch
each week.

### Run the match

[Match execution](match-execution.md) is built for a phone at the side of a
pitch, in the cold, with one hand. Substitutions and minutes go in as they
happen.

### Record the result and the minutes

After the match, enter the score and each player's minutes. If you did not use
the live screen, the [minutes grid](minutes-grid.md) takes a whole squad at
once.

Minutes are what let you answer *"am I actually spreading playing time?"* with
a number instead of an impression. That question comes up every season, usually
from a parent.

### Write the match analysis

[Match analysis](match-analysis.md) is where you say what you made of the game:
an overall read, then a rating and a few points per methodology team function,
with what your match plan asked for shown alongside.

Below that is the roster of everyone who played. You can mark a player as
**Stood out**, **As expected** or **Below par** and add one specific line. That
line lands on the player's own timeline as *Observed in a match* — which is how
a match on a Saturday becomes part of a development picture rather than a
result that scrolls away.

## Across the week

### Triage your dashboard

Your dashboard leads with what is next and what is waiting: evaluations due,
PDP reviews, pending acknowledgements, the next thing on the calendar. Clearing
it takes a couple of minutes and is the cheapest way to stop work piling up
into an evaluation-window scramble.

### Keep availability current

When a player is injured, ill or away, set it straight away — from your phone
if that is where you heard it. An [injury](injuries.md) is a dated record on the
player's journey, not a note that disappears.

Availability feeds the next attendance screen and your squad selection, so a
minute here saves you picking an unavailable player on Friday.

### Move PDPs and goals along

Add progress to a [goal](goals.md), close one that is done, or set a new one
off the back of an evaluation. The [PDP cycle](pdp-cycle.md) explains how the
talks and reflections fit together across a season.

### Talk to players and parents

Messages sent from inside a player's context are logged against that player's
record, so the next coach can see what was already said. That matters most
where it is least convenient: no-show follow-ups and difficult conversations.

## Every evaluation window

### Submit evaluations

Rate every category and add what you would actually tell the player. The
[new evaluation wizard](new-evaluation-wizard.md) walks a whole squad in one
sitting; the [ratings grid](ratings-grid.md) is faster if you already know your
scores.

Partial work is kept, so you can stop halfway and come back.

Read [evaluations](evaluations.md) for what the categories mean and how the
star ratings map to the academy's scale.

## Reading the squad

Every decision above — who starts, who gets a 1:1, what to drill next, when to
raise something with the head of development — comes from reading what you have
captured. The lenses worth knowing:

| Question | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Who is slipping on attendance? | [Reports](reports.md) |
| Is playing time actually spread? | [Reports](reports.md), minutes reports |
| Are we improving on what we drilled? | [Evaluations](evaluations.md) trends |
| Are PDP goals closing, or stacking up? | [PDP cycle](pdp-cycle.md) |
| Which of these two for the slot? | [Player comparison](player-comparison.md) |
| Who can cover this position? | [Team chemistry](team-chemistry.md) |

If a lens you want is not here, say so — the reports are shaped by what coaches
ask for.

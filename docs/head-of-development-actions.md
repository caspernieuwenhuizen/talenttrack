# Head of development — the season in actions

The head of development sees the whole academy rather than one team: the intake
funnel at one end, the promotion and release decisions at the other, and the
quality of what the coaches are capturing in between.

This page walks the recurring work in the order it tends to arrive. If you are
new here, read [Getting started](getting-started.md) first.

## Every day

### Triage the inbox and read the pulse

Your dashboard opens on the academy's numbers — active players, evaluations
this month, rolling attendance, open trial cases, PDP verdicts pending — and
then the team grid, with any team needing attention sorted to the front.

Below that: what is coming up across every team, trials waiting on a decision,
and your task inbox.

Two minutes on a phone in a car park is enough to know whether anything needs
you today. The deeper sweep is worth doing two or three times a week.

## The intake funnel

The funnel runs scout → you → trial → player. Each step hands you a task rather
than expecting you to remember.

### Invite a prospect to a test training

When a scout logs a prospect, a task lands on you. Open it and you get the
scout's discovery context and their notes without going looking.

Pick a test-training date that fits the right age group, draft the invitation
from a template, personalise it, and send it to the parent. Parents reply
through a signed link — no account, no password.

Speed matters more than polish here. A prospect invited within two days is
interested; a prospect invited in three weeks has usually been invited
somewhere else too.

You author the canonical invitation copy once in the trial letter templates
editor, so this stays a two-minute job rather than a writing exercise.

### Record the test-training outcome

The day after, a task arrives with the prospect already attached. Record
whether they came, how they did, and what happens next: admit to the trial
group, decline, or take a second look.

On admit, the trial case and the player record are created for you, and the
scout's original discovery context travels onto the player record — so in two
years you can still see who found this player and why.

The scout sees the outcome on their own pipeline without having to chase you.

### Review a trial player

Trial groups get a quarterly review. See [trials](trials.md) for how trial
cases, tracks and the synthesis fit together.

## Player-level judgement

### Walk a player's timeline

This is the flow the whole system exists to serve. Open a player and read the
timeline end to end: trial outcome, evaluations, PDPs, goals, minutes,
injuries, notes, team moves — in order.

You are looking for trajectory, not a snapshot. A 7 that used to be a 6 and a 7
that used to be an 8 are different players.

Then decide the intervention: talk to the coach, add a note, set a PDP goal,
change team, or escalate.

### Move a player between teams

An age-group move is a tracked event, not a quiet edit. Recording it as a
transition keeps the player's history readable — you can still see, next
season, when they went up and what happened after.

### Resolve a flagged team

When a team is flagged, it is usually one of two things: results that are
genuinely off, or capture that has stopped. Check attendance and evaluation
counts before assuming the first.

## Quality of what is captured

### Spot-check evaluations

During an evaluation window, sample submitted evaluations across teams. You are
checking that coaches are actually differentiating — a coach who gives every
player the same score has not evaluated anyone.

[Evaluation coverage](eval-coverage.md) shows who is unevaluated this window
and which coach owns the gap, which is faster than sampling blind.

### Author the quarterly team review

Each team gets a written review each quarter. Read
[development management](development-management.md) for how these fit into the
academy's development picture.

## Planning ahead

### Schedule the next test trainings

Test trainings are the top of the funnel; if they stop, intake stops about six
weeks later. Plan them from the [team planner](team-planner.md) alongside
everything else the age group is doing.

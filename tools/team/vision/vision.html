The academy's memory and its conscience.

Every academy has software, and all of it records. TalentTrack should be the first that makes an academy wiser: it remembers every player, makes every judgement fair, and learns from every decision the academy makes. Written from the academy owner's chair.

The problem

The decisions that matter most are still made on memory

At the end of a season, in a room, by the people who shouted loudest about the last six weeks. Three things go wrong, and every academy owner knows them.

  • We release the wrong children.

    A boy born in October, small at thirteen, looks behind a boy born in January who has already grown. We call it talent. It is a birth date and a growth spurt. Some of the players we let go would have been the best we ever had.

  • We cannot tell whether our coaches agree.

    One coach's 7 is another's 5. A player's file reads like progress or decline depending on who held the clipboard that month.

  • We do not learn.

    Nobody checks, three years later, what became of the players we released or kept. The same mistakes repeat every season, because the academy has no memory of its own decisions.

The software we use makes recording easier. None of it makes the academy wiser.

The vision
TalentTrack remembers every player, makes every judgement fair, and learns from every decision the academy makes.

The player stays at the centre. What changes is what the system does with what it holds. It stops being a filing cabinet and becomes a colleague that notices: this judgement is skewed by birth date, these two coaches disagree, this player is drifting away from the game, this decision looks like one we got wrong before.

Six bets

What no academy product does today

Each bet has a first step that builds on what TalentTrack already holds, so the vision is reached by shipping, not by a rewrite.

1

Fair judgement

Every rating shown beside the player's birth quarter and maturity estimate. A release candidate who is young for the cohort or late to grow is flagged, and training groups can be formed by maturity as well as by age.

Why it does not exist: the relative age effect is decades old in research and still absent from the screen where the decision is made.

First stepDated height measurements per player, and the birth quarter on the evaluation and the release decision.

2

Calibrated coaches

When five coaches rate the same players, the system sees how far apart they are. Each coach sees, privately, where their ratings sit against the staff. The head of development sees where a calibration session would help.

Why it does not exist: rating forms treat every number as truth. Nobody measures the measurer.

First stepA calibration report on existing evaluations, for the head of development only.

3

An academy that remembers its decisions

Every signing, promotion, release and graduation kept with its reasoning and evidence. A year later and three years later the system asks: where is this player now? The academy sees its own track record, misses included.

Why it does not exist: academies keep player records, not decision records, and nobody follows a released child.

First stepRelease and graduation events carry a reasoning and an evidence snapshot; a yearly "where are they now" review.

4

The coach speaks, the record writes itself

Fifteen seconds into the phone on the touchline: "Daan, good scanning before receiving, still drifts inside when we lose the ball." It becomes an observation on Daan's file, linked to the principle and his goal, confirmed with one tap.

Why it does not exist: academy software is built for the office. The observations worth keeping happen on grass.

First stepVoice notes on the mobile surfaces, transcribed into a suggested observation; nothing saved without the coach's tap.

5

Every goal has a moment on video

A goal says "first touch under pressure". The player sees three clips from Saturday where it went well and one where it did not, attached to the goal and shown in the development talk.

Why it does not exist: video tools and development tools live apart. Clips end up in a chat group, goals in a form.

First stepA clip link with a timestamp on a goal, an evaluation or a match-analysis note, shown in the PDP talk.

6

The whole child, and a dignified exit

A short check-in the player fills in (energy, mood, school load) shows who is struggling before it shows on the pitch. A released player leaves with their story: the record, the coaches' words and the clips, as a document that belongs to them.

Why it does not exist: academies are built around the players they keep. The ones they release vanish the day they leave.

First stepA player-owned export of the development record on release, built on the player report.

The guardrails

These are minors. Every bet stays inside these lines.

  • The coach decidesThe system notices, suggests and remembers. It never signs, releases or ranks a child on its own.
  • Every number explains itselfA flag says why it was raised, from which data, and how sure it is.
  • No public ranking of childrenComparisons stay inside the staff roles that need them.
  • Sensitive stays sensitiveWellbeing, growth and medical data are permission-gated and audit-logged.
  • The family sees its own storyIn language made for a player and their parents, not coach jargon.
From vision to roadmap

How the bets reach the roadmap

  1. The owner adopts the vision and picks which bets go first.
  2. Each chosen bet becomes an epic, with its first step as the first child, in the roadmap's Next column.
  3. The rest stay here until their turn. The vision is reviewed once per season.

Owner, 30 October 2026: the first bets are chosen when the ready queue is close to empty. Until then no bet enters the roadmap.
